# v0.5: construction speed

Prompted by outside feedback: `Float64` rules were too slow to be taken seriously by someone
used to pasting a table. First `rule(Simplex{2}(); degree = 10)` took 22 s (98% compilation),
degree 30 took 7.7 s, degree 40 29 s, a degree-15 tetrahedron 16 s; high-degree rules at
high precision ran to minutes, and the order-125 Lebedev rule at 100 digits to 760 s.

## Float64 is served from the tables

The stored seeds were already the refined rules rounded correctly to `Float64` (PROVENANCE
says "refined to 100 digits and rounded"). `scripts/certify_tables.jl` confirmed it: of 78
entries, 77 were correctly rounded to the last bit; the degree-11 triangle was 3 ulp off
and was replaced. The script also records each entry's residual at 256 bits (all below
1.3e-15). So at ≤ 53 bits a seeded family ships the stored rule, or rounds it for `Float32`
and `Float16` (whose residual is then evaluated in `Float64`). The certificate quotes the
recorded residual; the test suite recomputes every recorded residual (at 128 bits, 2 min).

The user decided against storing ~40-digit tables: above `Float64` a request needs Newton
iterations anyway, and 20 more stored digits would save about one of them.

Consequence worth knowing: the shipped `Float64` rule's parameters are identical to the
refined rule's, but the barycentric coordinate derived from them (`1 − Σ`) is computed from
the rounded parameters and can differ by an ulp from the correctly rounded coordinate of
the exact rule. The reproducibility hash of the `Float64` triangle case changed for that
reason.

Two costs hid behind the shipped path and had to be moved out of it: the invariant basis
(a Float64 Reynolds projection: 3 s for the degree-20 tetrahedron, now cached per `(N, n)`)
and `families()`, a `subtypes` walk of 50 ms done twice per `rule` (now cached per world
age, reset in `__init__`). After that: 1–2 ms per `Float64` request for every table family.

## Refinement above Float64

Profiled at triangle degree 40, 32 digits (24.9 s). In order of what was done:

1. **`Qᵀ J` with a dense `Q`.** `Q` is block diagonal by degree (96% zeros at degree 30);
   multiplying block by block made that product 15× cheaper. No SparseArrays: it costs
   0.33 s at `using`.
2. **BigFloat allocation, not arithmetic.** 45% of samples were `BigFloat()` and the
   `ScopedValue` lookup of the default precision that every allocating operation makes;
   `BigFloat * Int` cost 500 ns against 66 ns for an in-place `mpfr_mul`. The Dubiner
   kernels (triangle and tetrahedron) and the residual/Jacobian accumulation now use
   in-place MPFR calls with precomputed coefficients (`src/core/mpfr.jl`): 7.8× on the
   triangle kernel, results equal to the generic ones to the rounding unit.
3. **The linear solve.** The generic column-pivoted BigFloat QR (58% after step 2)
   recomputes every column norm at every step. For square systems with κ < 1e10 the step is
   now a `Float64` pivoted QR plus BigFloat iterative refinement (O(n²) per correction,
   ~16 − log κ digits each), matching the QR to 8e-59 at 200 bits in 0.03 s instead of 0.7 s.
   The `Float64` factorisation is written in plain loops, no BLAS, no SIMD reductions, so
   its bits do not depend on the machine: the refinement stays reproducible, which is why
   the pure-Julia QR was chosen in the first place.
4. **Ill-conditioned systems** (Lebedev: κ = 3e27 at degree 65, 9e54 at 125) keep an
   extended-precision factorisation, now an in-place pivoted Householder QR. The accurate
   condition estimate, a full BigFloat SVD at 256 bits twice per run (73% of a degree-65
   Lebedev build), is now that QR plus power and inverse iteration on `R`: O(n²) per step.
   It agreed with the SVD to five digits on the cases checked.
5. **Lebedev right-hand sides** were BigFloat log-gamma sums, recomputed for every system at
   every precision (a third of a degree-65 build). On `S²` every monomial moment is 4π times
   a rational, so each invariant moment is now an exact rational, computed once and cached.
   The first version still took 41% of a first degree-89 build, recomputing double
   factorials term by term; the terms of one moment share their denominator, so the sum is
   now kept in integers with a growing table of odd double factorials (0.1 s for all
   moments to degree 125).
6. **The in-place QR** updates column norms instead of recomputing them, recomputing only
   where cancellation has cost a quarter of the precision (the pivot column's norm, which
   the reflector uses, is always fresh), and applies each reflector with one fused
   operation per entry.
7. **Compilation.** A PrecompileTools workload over the common paths (`src/precompile.jl`)
   took the first `rule(Simplex{2}(); degree = 10)` in a new session from 22 s to 0.15 s, at
   the cost of about 40 s more precompilation and 0.3 s more `using`.

What is left in the degree-125 Lebedev build is six Newton iterations of about 10 s each (a
352 × 352 QR at 550 bits and the line search), three of which are damped steps from the
`Float64` seed before quadratic convergence sets in. Running those early iterations at a
lower precision would be the next saving.

The tetrahedron is now dominated by real MPFR work: 1771 basis functions with gradients at
441 nodes. Evaluating one node per orbit instead of all of them would cut that by up to
24×, but only with an exactly invariant basis; the current `Q` is a Float64 projection,
invariant only to 1e-16, which is harmless for `Qᵀ r = 0` but not for the shortcut.

## Traps met on the way

- `BigFloat(x; precision = p)` returns `x` itself when `x` already has precision `p`. A
  "copy" made that way and then written in place overwrote the caller's Jacobian; the QR
  looked plausible and was wrong. Copies for in-place work go through `mp_set!` into a new
  number.
- `zeros(BigFloat, n)` fills with one shared object. `unshare!` (used at the entry of every
  kernel) replaces repeated entries; its first version compared each entry with the
  already-replaced previous one and left every other entry shared.
- In-place MPFR results are rounded to the precision of the destination, so buffers are
  created inside the `with_bits` scope that uses them.
- A local function defined in both branches of an `if` is hoisted: the second definition
  always wins. Use anonymous functions assigned to a variable.
