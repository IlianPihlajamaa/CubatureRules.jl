Ball

Domains

CubatureRules.Ball — Type
Ball{D}()
Ball(centre, radius)

The solid ball of dimension D: Ball{3}() is the unit ball in three-space, Ball{2}() the unit disk. Ball{D}() is the reference ball, centred at the origin with radius 1, and its measure is the volume π^{D/2} / Γ(D/2 + 1) · r^D.

Ball product

CubatureRules.BallProduct — Type
BallProduct(angular = SphereProduct())

Product rules on a Ball: a Gauss–Jacobi rule in the radius against the sphere family angular on the surface. With n = ⌈(d+1)/2⌉ radial points it has n times the angular rule's node count, all interior, all positive.

BallProduct(LebedevRule()) is the cheap one wherever a Lebedev seed exists; the selector offers both and ranks them by node count.

source

A ball separates into a radius and a sphere:

\[\int_{B^D} f \, dx = \int_0^1 r^{D-1} \int_{S^{D-1}} f(r\omega) \, d\sigma(\omega) \, dr .\]

A polynomial of degree d restricted to a ray is r^d times a polynomial on the sphere, so the two factors can be treated separately: a Gauss–Jacobi rule for the weight r^{D-1} in the radius, and any sphere family for the angles. The selector tries every sphere family as the angular factor, so Ball{3}() gets a Lebedev angular rule where a Lebedev seed exists and a product rule elsewhere.

npoints(rule(Ball{3}(); degree = 9)), npoints(rule(BallProduct(SphereProduct()), Ball{3}(); degree = 9))
(190, 250)

Rules on a ball are verified against monomials with exact moments. Unlike on the sphere, the coordinates in a ball satisfy no relation, so the monomials are a basis. Their moments are those of the sphere divided by |α| + D.
