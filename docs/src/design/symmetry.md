# Orbit algebra

The smallest rules on the triangle, tetrahedron and sphere are symmetric: they are unchanged
by the symmetry group of the domain. The package uses that structure both to search for such
rules and to refine them.

## Orbits

A symmetric rule consists of whole *orbits*: a point together with all its images under the
group, sharing one weight. A rule is then described by a list of orbit types and, for each
orbit, a weight and a few parameters that fix the representative point. This is the
*structure* of the rule, and the parameters are the unknowns that the refinement solves for.

### Simplices

On a `D`-simplex the group is `S_{D+1}`, which permutes the barycentric coordinates. An
orbit type is a pattern of equal coordinates. On the triangle there are three:

| Type | Representative | Points | Parameters |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[3]` | `(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)` | 1 | 0 |
| `[2,1]` | `(a, a, 1-2a)` | 3 | 1 |
| `[1,1,1]` | `(a, b, 1-a-b)` | 6 | 2 |

The tetrahedron has five orbit types under `S₄`, from the centroid (1 point) to the general
orbit (24 points).

### The sphere

On the sphere in `ℝ³` the group is the octahedral group `O_h`: the 48 signed permutations of
the coordinates. Its orbits are the six used by Lebedev:

| Type | Representative | Points | Parameters |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a₁` | `(1, 0, 0)` | 6 | 0 |
| `a₂` | `(1, 1, 0)/√2` | 12 | 0 |
| `a₃` | `(1, 1, 1)/√3` | 8 | 0 |
| `b` | `(l, l, m)`, `m² = 1 - 2l²` | 24 | 1 |
| `c` | `(p, q, 0)`, `q² = 1 - p²` | 24 | 1 |
| `d` | `(r, s, u)`, `u² = 1 - r² - s²` | 48 | 2 |

## The moment equations

A rule made of whole orbits is invariant under the group. Its integration error on a
polynomial `p` therefore equals its error on the group average of `p`. Every polynomial whose
group average is zero is integrated exactly, whatever the parameters are. So only the
invariant polynomials give equations, and there are far fewer of them than polynomials of
the same degree.

### On simplices

The equations are projected onto the invariant subspace of each degree block of an
orthonormal basis. That subspace is the range of the Reynolds (group-averaging) operator,
and its dimension is checked against the Molien series of the group. The resulting system
has exactly as many equations as independent invariant moments.

The invariant basis is computed in `Float64`. An inexact basis only affects the conditioning,
not the solution, because the residual of a symmetric rule already lies in the invariant
subspace.

### On the sphere

The invariant polynomials of `O_h` on `ℝ³` are generated by `p₂ = Σxᵢ²`, `p₄ = Σxᵢ⁴` and
`p₆ = x²y²z²`. On the sphere `p₂ = 1`, so the invariant polynomials of degree at most `n`
are spanned by `p₄^a p₆^b` with `4a + 6b ≤ n`. At degree 131 that gives a few hundred
equations, where the full set of spherical harmonics would give 17 424.

Because an invariant polynomial has the same value at every point of an orbit, each orbit
only needs to be evaluated at its representative. With the constraint substituted, `p₄` and
`p₆` are polynomials in the orbit parameters, so the equations contain no square roots.

Every orbit is centrally symmetric, so odd-degree polynomials integrate to zero for any
parameters. A rule exact to degree `2k` is therefore exact to `2k + 1`, and Lebedev rules are
only offered at odd degrees.

## Conditioning of the invariant basis

The basis `p₄^a p₆^b` is badly conditioned, for the same reason monomials are: the condition
number of its Gram matrix grows by about 0.83 decades per degree (about `10¹¹` at degree 17,
`10²¹` at degree 29). This shows up as a large `cond` in the certificates of high-degree
Lebedev rules, and is covered by extra guard digits.

Orthonormalising the basis (via a Cholesky factor of its Gram matrix) was tried and makes
the linear solve much better conditioned, but does not reduce the working precision needed,
because the residual is still formed in the original basis. It was slower overall and was
not adopted. The measurements are recorded in `notes/v0.4-spheres.md`.

## Recovering the structure of a rule

`CubatureRules.classify_octahedral(points, weights)` decomposes a set of points on the
sphere into `O_h` orbits, or reports that this is impossible. This is how a rule from
another source, such as Lebedev.jl, is converted into orbit parameters so it can be refined.
